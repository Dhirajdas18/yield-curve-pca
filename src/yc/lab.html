The three shapes

How to read it: each line shows how much every maturity’s yield moves when one component moves by one unit. A flat line (level) moves the whole curve up or down together; a rising line (slope) moves long yields one way and short yields the other; a bent line (curvature) moves the middle against both ends.

How much each component explains

How to read it: the share of all the yield movement in the years you picked that each component accounts for. The first three usually add up to well over 90%.

The components over time

How to read it: on monthly changes, each line adds up the month’s moves along one shape, so a falling level line means yields fell across the curve and a rising slope line means the curve got steeper. Units are the shape’s own: one unit of level moves each of ten yields by about a third of a percentage point. On yield levels, each line is the curve’s position along that shape.
